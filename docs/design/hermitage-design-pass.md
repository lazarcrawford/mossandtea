# Hermitage Client Lounge Design Pass

## Source Studied

The requested VoltAgent reference is `VoltAgent/awesome-design-md`. For this pass, the repository was cloned locally to `/tmp/awesome-design-md` and reviewed as a pattern library for agent-readable design systems.

Applied lessons:

- Strong `DESIGN.md` files are explicit about tokens, component anatomy, responsive behavior, and anti-patterns.
- Photography-first systems such as Apple and Airbnb keep chrome quiet so the image carries the emotional signal.
- Workspace systems such as Notion make status, next action, and navigation visible without making the experience feel like a dashboard dump.
- Accent colors should behave like signals, not decoration.
- Motion and layout should clarify state rather than advertise the interface.

This pass does not copy VoltAgent's brand. It translates those product-system instincts into Moss & Tea's photographic, editorial language.

## Name

Use **Hermitage** as the client portal name.

Positioning:

> Hermitage is the Moss & Tea client lounge: a premium, white-glove service layer for private galleries, selections, service requests, archive moments, and future Shopify-powered keepsakes.

Avoid "exclusive" as the primary idea. The tone is private and elevated, but not status-driven.

## Voice

Hermitage should sound like a trusted studio host.

Use:

- "Your gallery is ready for a quiet first pass."
- "Irina has prepared a suggested set to begin with."
- "These photographs can become finished pieces when you are ready."
- "Review, choose, request, and return when the work is ready."

Avoid:

- "Exclusive access."
- "Limited-time offer."
- "Upsell", "bundle", "deal", or other marketplace language.
- Explaining internal software mechanics to the client.

## Visual Direction

The portal should feel more operational than the public site, but still art-led.

- Foundation: dark ink, porcelain, bronze, moss, wine, and blue-black.
- Typography: Cormorant Garamond for atmosphere and Inter for utility.
- Shape: 8px or less radius, thin borders, restrained panels.
- Imagery: real Moss & Tea photography always leads.
- UI density: calmer than admin, more functional than the public homepage.

## Milestone Design Versions

### V0 / MVP: Private Gallery and Project Room

Primary feeling: secure, calm, and clear.

Client sees login, project status, gallery, favorites, final picks, documents, billing links, and notes. The first screen answers what is ready and what happens next.

### Polished MVP: Image-Level Paid Services

Primary feeling: service is close to the image.

The image action menu introduces retouching, print-ready files, social crops, rush exports, and custom notes without turning the gallery into a storefront.

### V1: Complete Client Operations Lounge

Primary feeling: no manual handoff required.

Hermitage becomes the home for lifecycle status, proofing, payments, contracts, revision status, service orders, and support notes.

### V2: Archive Intelligence

Primary feeling: the archive remembers.

Past projects return through anniversary moments, seasonal mini-collections, saved favorites, campaign preferences, and gentle reminders of images with future value.

### V3: Commerce Bridge

Primary feeling: product desire is proven by curation.

Clients can preview or request fine-art prints, cards, albums, framed work, digital packs, and gift products. Quote paths stay human-led until demand and fulfillment quality are proven.

### V4: Agentic Shopify Merchandising

Primary feeling: automation prepares, the studio approves.

Agents generate recommendations, mockups, product descriptions, campaign copy, and Shopify carts, but admin review remains the quality gate.

### V5: Full Shopify Client Lounge

Primary feeling: a personalized showroom over the private archive.

The mature Hermitage experience includes client-specific Shopify storefront surfaces, crop and material controls, cart or checkout handoff, order history, and fulfillment status returning to the portal.

Supabase remains the client, project, permission, and archive source of truth. Shopify owns checkout, tax, shipping, receipts, products, variants, and fulfillment integrations.
